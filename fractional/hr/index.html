Fractional HR leadership

You have a team and nobody whose job is people.

So it lands on you. The hiring, the handbook nobody has read, the conversation you have been putting off for three weeks. I step into that seat one day a week, take it off your desk, and build it so your managers can hold it without me.

What you are actually buying

A day of my week, inside your business.

MonTueWedThuFri

Not a policy binder, and not a compliance review that ends with a list of things you are supposed to go fix yourself. I take the work. The offer letter, the onboarding nobody owns, the review cycle you keep meaning to start, the termination you have been rehearsing in the car.

Then I hand it to your managers with the words already written down, because a people function that only works when I am in the building is not a function. It is a dependency.

Who this is for

Owners of businesses with a team, roughly ten to fifty people, where HR is currently whoever has time. If you already have an HR manager and you want a second opinion, that is a different and much smaller conversation, and I will tell you so on the call.

The part nobody wants to talk about

The exposure is almost never the handbook. It is the manager who says one wrong sentence in a termination. It is the complaint that got handled in a hallway and never written down. It is a file that would not survive a lawyer reading it line by line.

I am not a lawyer, and I will tell you plainly the moment you need one. What I do is make sure that by the time you need one, the paperwork is already on your side and your managers already know which sentence not to say.

The job is to make the job go away

Most retainers are built to grow. This one is built to shrink.

I am not trying to become the person you cannot operate without. Your managers end up holding this, and my time on your calendar goes down every quarter until you drop a tier, hand it to somebody permanent, or tell me we are done.

Months 1 to 3 The heaviest stretch. We fix what is actually exposed, get the basics written down, and stop the bleeding on whatever is costing you people.
Months 4 to 6 Your managers run the conversations. I sit in on the hard ones, review the documentation, and correct what needs correcting.
Month 7 onward You step down a tier, or you hire the person who takes this from me and I help you pick them. I will tell you when I think it is time rather than wait for you to ask.

Hours run high while we are building and taper once it runs. That is why the tiers step down instead of the invoice staying flat forever.

Why this is not just a smaller salary

An HR manager is a real hire. Salary, then benefits and payroll taxes on top of it, which is commonly put at somewhere near 1.4 times the salary once you add everything up. Then the months before they know your business. Then the severance conversation if it does not work, which is a particularly uncomfortable one to have with the person who handles severance.

This is a variable cost. You turn it up during a hiring push or a mess, down once it runs, and off when you are done. No headcount to approve, no ramp, no benefits, no severance. That is the trade, and it is a different shape of cost rather than a discount on the same one.

The first 30 days

You should be able to tell whether this was worth it before the second invoice.

  • Week 1. I talk to you and to the managers who actually carry people problems day to day. I am looking for what is exposed and what is costing you people, not building a compliance checklist.
  • Week 2. You get it in writing. What is exposed, what it would cost you if it went wrong, and which one we fix first. If the honest answer is that you need a payroll provider and not me, the document will say that, and you keep the document either way.
  • Weeks 3 and 4. We start fixing. One real thing is working before the first month is out, and the manager who owns it has been walked through it rather than emailed a template.

Do not hire me if

  • You want a handbook and nothing else. Buy a template. It will cost you a few hundred dollars and it will sit in the same drawer as the last one.
  • You are a one person shop. There has to be a team for any of this to matter.
  • You want someone to make a problem employee disappear without doing it properly. I will not, and you would not want the version of me that would.
  • You want this handled quietly, around your managers. I train them. That is not a bonus, it is the deliverable.
  • You need someone to run payroll and benefits administration. That is a provider, not a person, and I will point you at one.

What I am not going to show you

A wall of client logos. I am early in this on my own, and I would rather you hear that from me than work it out on your own.

What I will tell you is that I have sat on the other side of an investigation, a termination and a layoff. I know what a file needs to look like before a lawyer reads it, and I know the difference between a conversation that solves something and one that just creates a record.

And I run my own business on a system I built myself. Not a demo. The actual thing, live, on the call, with the rough edges still in it. If that is not enough proof for you, I would rather you pass now than be disappointed in month three.

How many of these I can hold

A handful at a time. That is arithmetic and not a sales line: at a day a week each, I run out of week. When I am full I will tell you I am full, and I will tell you roughly when that changes.
